Method and limits

Corpus. OpenAI's openai/math Lean library (release of October 6, 2026) and the earlier openai/ten-proofs. Controls: mathlib at the exact revision OpenAI pins; four human-written research formalizations (Carleson, FLT, PFR, sphere eversion) at their current commits; StrongPNT (Math Inc.) as a second AI-produced corpus. Lean core and Batteries are used only to resolve names.

Parsing. No elaboration (mathlib cannot be built on the analysis machine), so this is a syntactic parse: comments and strings stripped; namespaces, sections and open tracked; declarations split into statement and proof; every identifier resolved against the declared names of all corpora, with local binders shadowing globals, generated names (structure fields, constructors, to_additive translations) included, and field notation h.foo resolved only when foo is unique in the corpus and absent from mathlib. Declaration counts match an independent line count on random samples. Dependency counts are lower bounds.

Units. A project is a directory (OpenAI: OAI/<Area>/<Topic>; mathlib: Mathlib/<A>/<B>; human projects: first-level subdirectory). A headline result's cone is every same-corpus declaration reachable from it through statement or proof references.

Attribute families. Concept formation (how many definitions a development introduces and whether they become shared vocabulary), brute force (certificate mass, case enumeration, tactic mix), naming (length, serial numbers, placeholder words, mathlib vocabulary, how much a name is read off its statement, and whether a concept's name propagates into theorems about it), compression (duplicate and templated proofs, window-normalized zlib ratio, proof/statement length) and derivation (how much is built from library constants rather than local ones, and verbatim re-derivation across projects).

  • These are style proxies, not a verdict on whether a proof is explanatory. Validating them needs an outside signal: blinded mathematician ratings, the repository's withdrawals and repairs, or later reuse.
  • The Lean was produced by a formalization pipeline after the fact, so it measures formal-proof style, which may differ from the papers' arguments.
  • Human controls are small and may include some AI-assisted code; mathlib is a library, not a proof, so its single-use-lemma and proof-length numbers reflect that genre.
  • Field notation is under-resolved, more so in human code: the sphere-eversion cone is clearly incomplete. A detector for restated mathlib theorems was dropped because its hits were mostly false positives; true restatement is rare in every group.
